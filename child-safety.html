AGIK · Meta-Consensus Protocol

Child Safety Consensus on Blockchain

Multiple AI observers evaluate each interaction independently. When they converge, the verdict is sealed with a cryptographic hash on Tezos. When they don't — a human decides.

All scenarios below are synthetic. They were written by the architect (a parent and educator) to demonstrate the system's behavior. No real child data was used, collected, or processed. This is a design principle, not a disclaimer — DSEI Principle V (data minimization) and GDPR-K compliance require it.
For non-technical readers: Imagine three independent referees watching the same conversation between a child and an AI tutor. Each referee scores the interaction on safety, age-appropriateness, and manipulation risk — without seeing the other referees' scores. If all three agree it's safe, the verdict is locked on a public ledger that nobody can edit. If any referee flags a problem, the conversation is paused and a human adult is called. The locked verdicts are what makes this auditable — not a company's promise, but math anyone can verify.

How Meta-Consensus works

01
Observe — Each AI observer independently evaluates the interaction across 6 safety pillars
02
Score — Confidence scores per pillar. Disagreement above threshold triggers review
03
Converge — If ≥2/3 observers agree: verdict is sealed. If not: human escalation
04
Anchor — SHA-256 hash of verdict + reasoning sealed on Tezos mainnet, immutable
